## 1. Overview

The National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET UG) is India's national entrance examination for admission to undergraduate medical and certain related courses. The National Testing Agency (NTA), under the Ministry of Education, conducts the examination.

The 2026 NTA bulletin identifies NEET as the common entrance test for MBBS and BDS, and for undergraduate Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy courses. NEET scores are also used by specified institutions and authorities for certain BSc Nursing admissions and other notified programmes. These additional uses do not mean that every BSc Nursing or allied-health programme in India admits students through NEET.

## 2. Who can apply?

The points below summarise the latest available **2026** NTA rules. The 2027 bulletin may change them.

### Educational qualification

For the usual Class 12 route, candidates must have passed or be appearing in a recognised qualifying examination with:

- Physics
- Chemistry
- Biology or Biotechnology
- English

The NTA bulletin also lists other recognised qualifying examination routes and permits candidates who studied the required subjects as additional subjects after Class 12 to apply, subject to the stated conditions and documentary proof.

Candidates whose Class 12 result is awaited may appear in NEET. However, they must pass the qualifying examination by the applicable counselling/admission stage to be eligible for admission.

### Age limit

Under the 2026 rules:

- The candidate must be at least **17 years old on or before 31 December of the admission year**.
- There is **no upper age limit** under the cited NMC clarification.

For the 2027 cycle, the corresponding date and rule must be checked in the 2027 bulletin.

### Number of attempts

The 2026 bulletin does not specify a maximum number of NEET attempts. A separate 2027 attempt rule is **Not verified** until the next bulletin is released.

### Nationality

Indian citizens, NRIs, OCIs and foreign nationals may be eligible, subject to the applicable government, state, institution and counselling rules. NRI/OCI/foreign candidates may need to upload supporting nationality or status documents.

## 4. Application process: step by step

The 2027 application portal and instructions are **Not announced**. The following is a practical outline based on the 2026 NTA process; follow the 2027 bulletin when released.

1. Visit the [official NEET website](https://neet.nta.nic.in/).
2. Open the NEET UG 2027 registration link when NTA activates it.
3. Read the Information Bulletin and check eligibility before registering.
4. Register using the details and contact information requested by NTA. Use an active email address and mobile number.
5. Complete the online application with personal, academic, category and other required details.
6. Upload the photograph, signature and other documents in the format and size specified in the live instructions.
7. Select the examination cities and question-paper language from the available options.
8. Pay the examination fee online using an available payment method.
9. Submit the form and confirm that the confirmation page is generated.
10. Download and keep the confirmation page and payment receipt.

Do not submit multiple applications. Check every entry carefully because corrections may be limited to the fields and dates specified by NTA.

## 6. Exam pattern

The latest available 2026 bulletin specifies the following pattern. The 2027 pattern is **Not announced**.

| Subject                      | Questions |   Marks |
| ---------------------------- | --------: | ------: |
| Physics                      |        45 |     180 |
| Chemistry                    |        45 |     180 |
| Biology (Botany and Zoology) |        90 |     360 |
| **Total**                    |   **180** | **720** |

- **Question type:** Multiple-choice questions with four options and one correct/best answer.
- **Mode:** Offline, pen-and-paper examination using an OMR answer sheet.
- **Duration:** 180 minutes (3 hours) in 2026.
- **Maximum marks:** 720 in 2026.
- **2026 marking scheme:** +4 for each correct answer, −1 for each incorrect answer, and 0 for an unanswered question.
- **Languages:** The 2026 bulletin listed English, Hindi and specified regional languages. Check the 2027 bulletin for the final language and centre availability.

## 7. Syllabus summary

The 2026 syllabus was finalised by the Undergraduate Medical Education Board of the National Medical Commission and published through NTA. The 2027 syllabus is **Not announced**.

The broad subject areas are:

### Physics

Core areas include mechanics, laws of motion, work and energy, rotational motion, gravitation, properties of matter, thermodynamics, oscillations and waves, electrostatics, current electricity, magnetism, electromagnetic induction, optics, modern physics and electronic devices.

### Chemistry

The syllabus covers physical, inorganic and organic chemistry. Areas include atomic structure, chemical bonding, thermodynamics, equilibrium, solutions, electrochemistry, chemical kinetics, periodic properties, coordination compounds, hydrocarbons, organic functional groups and biomolecules.

### Biology

The syllabus includes diversity in the living world, structural organisation, cell biology, plant physiology, human physiology, reproduction, genetics and evolution, biology and human welfare, biotechnology, and ecology and environment.

**Preparation advice:** Use the official syllabus PDF rather than relying only on a coaching institute's topic list. Download the latest version from the [NTA NEET documents page](https://neet.nta.nic.in/documents/).

## 8. Preparation tips

1. **Start with the official syllabus.** Make a checklist of every topic in Physics, Chemistry and Biology.
2. **Build a consistent study routine.** Divide available study time across all three subjects and revisit difficult topics regularly.
3. **Use NCERT carefully.** Study the relevant NCERT material and verify that each topic is included in the current official syllabus.
4. **Practise questions after learning a topic.** Review both correct and incorrect answers to understand the concepts.
5. **Revise regularly.** Maintain short notes for formulas, reactions, definitions and Biology facts.
6. **Take timed mock tests.** Practise completing the paper within the official examination duration.
7. **Analyse mock-test performance.** Identify whether errors come from concept gaps, calculation mistakes, misreading or time management.
8. **Avoid unverified syllabus claims.** Use NTA and NMC notices for official changes.

## 10. Counselling process

Candidates must register separately for the counselling processes relevant to the seats and courses they want. NTA conducts the examination and declares the result; it does not allot college seats.

### MCC counselling

The Medical Counselling Committee (MCC), under the Directorate General of Health Services, conducts counselling for the categories of seats assigned to it. The 2026 bulletin included:

- 15% All India Quota (AIQ) MBBS and BDS seats in participating government colleges.
- 100% seats in specified central institutions and universities, including AIIMS and JIPMER MBBS seats, and specified BHU, AMU and other central-institution seats.
- Deemed university seats, including applicable NRI seats.
- Specified ESIC and other institutional seats.
- Registration for AFMC, with further selection handled by AFMC.
- 100% BSc Nursing seats in selected central BSc Nursing institutions, as specified in the bulletin.

The exact institutions, seat categories and process can change each year. Check the [MCC UG counselling portal](https://mcc.nic.in/ug-medical-counselling/).

### State counselling

State and Union Territory counselling authorities conduct counselling for seats under their control, including state quota seats and applicable private-college seats. Candidates must check the relevant state's eligibility, domicile, reservation, document and registration rules.

For example, a candidate applying for Karnataka state quota seats should follow the official Karnataka counselling authority's NEET UG notices. State eligibility and domicile rules are not automatically the same as MCC rules.

### AYUSH counselling

For AIQ seats in BAMS, BSMS, BUMS and BHMS, the 2026 NTA bulletin identifies the **Ayush Admissions Central Counselling Committee (AACCC)** as the counselling authority for the relevant government, government-aided, central, national and deemed institutions. State authorities handle state quota seats, while the relevant designated authority handles private AYUSH AIQ seats.

Official portal: [AACCC](https://aaccc.gov.in/)

### Understanding seat quotas

- **15% AIQ:** For the applicable All India Quota seats in participating state government medical and dental colleges, counselled by MCC.
- **State quota:** The remaining state-controlled seats are generally handled by the respective state authority. State rules determine eligibility and reservation.
- **Central institutions and deemed universities:** Seat allocation and counselling depend on the institution and seat category listed in the official MCC process.
- **AYUSH seats:** Counselling is handled through AACCC and state authorities, as applicable.

Do not assume every college follows the same quota split. Institution type, course, seat category and current government rules matter.

## 12. Cutoff trends

A single NEET cutoff number does not apply to every college. Candidates should distinguish between:

1. **Qualifying cutoff:** The minimum percentile needed to qualify for the relevant category.
2. **Admission closing rank:** The rank of the last candidate allotted a particular course, college, category and quota in a particular counselling round.

### Official qualifying criteria: 2026

The 2026 NTA bulletin specified the following qualifying percentiles for MBBS/BDS admission:

| Category                   | Qualifying percentile in 2026 |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------: |
| General / General-EWS      |               50th percentile |
| SC / ST / OBC-NCL          |               40th percentile |
| General / General-EWS PwBD |               45th percentile |
| SC / ST / OBC-NCL PwBD     |               40th percentile |

These are percentiles, not fixed marks. The corresponding marks can change with the examination and result distribution.

The 2026 bulletin also specified 50th percentile for General candidates and 40th percentile for SC/ST/OBC candidates for the listed BAMS, BSMS, BUMS and BHMS courses, with the applicable PwBD provisions.

## 13. Frequently asked questions

### 1. Is NEET UG 2027 conducted by NTA?

The latest available official information confirms that NTA conducted NEET UG 2026. The 2027 conducting-body notification is **Not announced** as of 24 November 2026. Check the official NEET website for the 2027 bulletin.

### 2. Can I apply for NEET if I am appearing for Class 12?

Under the 2026 rules, candidates awaiting their Class 12 result could apply and appear. They had to pass the qualifying examination by the applicable counselling/admission stage. Check the 2027 bulletin for confirmation.

### 3. Is there an upper age limit or a limit on attempts?

The 2026 bulletin stated that there was no upper age limit. It did not specify a maximum number of attempts. The corresponding 2027 rules are **Not verified** until the 2027 bulletin is released.

### 4. Does qualifying NEET guarantee an MBBS seat?

No. Qualifying makes a candidate eligible to participate in the applicable admission process, subject to other conditions. A seat depends on rank, category, quota, choices, seat availability and counselling rules.

### 5. Should I register for MCC and state counselling separately?

If you want to be considered for both MCC seats and state-controlled seats, you generally need to follow and register for each applicable counselling process separately. Check each authority's official instructions, deadlines and eligibility conditions.
